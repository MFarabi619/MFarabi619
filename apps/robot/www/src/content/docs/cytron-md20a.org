#+title: Cytron MD20A
#+description: Single-channel 20 A DC motor driver.
#+icon: CircuitBoard

[[https://www.cytron.io/p-20amp-6v-30v-dc-motor-driver][Cytron MD20A]] — a
single-channel bidirectional driver for one brushed DC motor, 20 A continuous
(60 A peak). Discrete NMOS H-bridge, no heatsink needed.

* Board layout

[[/cytron-md20a/board-layout.webp]]

| Component             | Detail                                        |
|-----------------------+-----------------------------------------------|
| Power input terminal  | ~VB+~ / ~VB−~                                 |
| Motor output terminal | ~MA~ / ~MB~                                    |
| PWM/DIR input         | ~DIR~ / ~PWM~ / ~GND~                          |
| Power LED             | On when powered                               |
| Motor status LEDs     | ~MA~ forward, ~MB~ backward                    |
| Overcurrent LED       | On while current limiting is active           |
| Error LED             | On during undervoltage shutdown or fault      |
| Test buttons          | ~MA~ forward / ~MB~ backward, at full speed    |

* Control interface

Three pins on the ~PWM/DIR~ header drive the one motor:

| Pin | Function                          |
|-----+-----------------------------------|
| DIR | Direction — static high / low     |
| PWM | Speed — square wave, up to 20 kHz |
| GND | Shared ground with the controller |

Logic inputs accept a wide 1.5–15 V, so a Raspberry Pi or Arduino GPIO drives it
directly.

* Truth table

Motor direction also depends on how ~MA~/~MB~ are wired — swap them to reverse.

| PWM  | DIR        | Output MA | Output MB | Motor    |
|------+------------+-----------+-----------+----------|
| Low  | don't care | Low       | Low       | Brake    |
| High | Low        | High      | Low       | Forward  |
| High | High       | Low       | High      | Backward |

* Electrical limits

Absolute maximums — exceed and you damage the board.

| # | Parameter                     | Min | Max | Unit |
|---+-------------------------------+-----+-----+------|
| 1 | Power input voltage           |   6 |  30 | V    |
| 2 | Continuous motor current      |   – |  20 | A    |
| 3 | Peak motor current[fn:1]      |   – |  60 | A    |
| 4 | Logic input — low level       |   0 | 0.8 | V    |
| 5 | Logic input — high level      | 1.5 |  15 | V    |
| 6 | PWM frequency — standard      |  DC |  20 | kHz  |
| 7 | PWM frequency — extended[fn:2] |  20 |  40 | kHz  |

[fn:1] Limited by the overcurrent circuit; the value shown is at 25–30 °C. The
limit drops as the board heats up.

[fn:2] Running in the extended range reduces the continuous current rating.

* Protection

- *Overcurrent* — active current limiting chops the PWM to hold current at the
  limit, so a stall or oversized motor won't destroy the board.
- *Temperature* — the current-limit threshold falls as the board heats up.
- *Undervoltage* — the output shuts down when the supply sags too low to drive
  the MOSFETs fully.

There is *no reverse-polarity protection* — wiring ~VB+~/~VB−~ backwards damages
the driver instantly.

* Mechanical

86 × 52 mm, with 81 × 47 mm between the four ⌀3 mm mounting holes.
